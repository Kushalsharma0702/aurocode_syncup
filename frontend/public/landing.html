The platform

A client portal with something actually happening inside it

Feedback tools capture well then go quiet. Portals own the relationship but give the client nothing to do. SyncUp is the loop that joins them.

How it works

Four steps, and the last one belongs to your client

The loop closing in front of them is the part that builds trust — and the part nobody else ships.

01 — CLIENT

They point

Click the element that's wrong, type one sentence. Screenshot, URL and click position attach themselves.

02 — SYNCUP

It becomes work

A real task on your board, tagged as feedback. You're notified; they're told when to expect an update.

03 — YOU

You fix it

Work it like anything else. Mark it done and the client is told it's live, with a link back to the thing.

04 — CLIENT

They confirm

One tap: Looks fixed, or reopen it. Either way it's closed, timestamped and on the record.

Why it exists

Feedback arrives broken. Then you pay to reassemble it.

Three costs every project with a non-technical buyer pays, every week.

Translation loss

A client describes a problem in words. You work out which page, which element, which screen size, which state. Every screenshot is a small decoding job.

“The thing at the top looks squished on my phone”

Scattered across five apps

Feedback forks the moment two people reply. Nothing is anchored to the live page, so version 3 arrives through a different channel than version 2 — and gets missed.

WhatsAppEmailVoice noteScreenshot.pngPhone call“as discussed”

Status anxiety

They can't see progress, so they ask. Asking costs you a context switch; not answering costs them confidence. Nobody enjoys the Friday update email.

“Just checking in — any update on this?”
